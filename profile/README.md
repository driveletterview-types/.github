# DriveLetterView - Letters for Disks on This PC

DriveLetterView is a table of volumes Windows has mounted on this machine. You use it to see which letter belongs to which disk. Looking does not write a partition.

---

## Product Snapshot

A USB stick can receive a different letter every time. A copy aimed at the old letter can land on another disk that now has that letter. The table is how you check. Read the label and the size. If they do not match the drive you hold, do not copy.

Network drives and card readers appear too. A letter with no disk is not a place to send files. This tool will not remove encryption. If a volume asks for a key you do not have, stop. It also will not partition or wipe. Do that only in a tool you opened on purpose, on a disk you own, after a backup.

## Highlights

- Letters currently mounted on this PC
- Type and label for each volume
- A refresh after you plug or unplug
- Size as a check against the hardware
- No format and no unlock
- Your machine's view only

---

## Live Production

Before a large copy, open the list, plug in the destination, refresh, and read the new letter. Then point the copy at that letter. When you eject, refresh again so a later job does not reuse a dead path.

On a shared PC, the list can show disks other people plugged in. Do not open those. Eject yours and close the tool.

---

## Refresh After the Click

Plug the disk in, refresh, and only then read the letter. Eject it, refresh, and confirm the letter left. A copy that still targets the old letter is aimed at whatever disk has that letter now.

---

## Setup Path

Prerequisites: Disks you are allowed to see on this PC.

1. Open DriveLetterView before you plug in the stick.
2. Plug in the disk you own and refresh.
3. Match the label and the size.
4. Use that letter for the copy.
5. Eject, refresh, and confirm the letter is gone.

## Who Benefits

DriveLetterView fits anyone who copies to removable disks and needs the current letter. It does not fit formatting, unlocking a drive you cannot open, or browsing a disk that is not yours.

---

## Fixes

The stick does not appear: try another cable or port, then refresh. The letter changed: do not use the old path. A volume asks for a key: stop. Two disks look alike: unplug one and refresh.
